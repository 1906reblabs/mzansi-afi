§ 01
Executive Brief

The COFI Bill is arriving this week in all but name. Deputy Commissioner Astrid Ludin exits the FSCA on 31 May 2026 after seven years overseeing the conduct compliance framework that COFI will replace. Commissioner Kamlana's second five-year mandate begins 1 June with one unfinished piece of business. At the March 2026 FSCA Conference, Kamlana confirmed National Treasury has indicated Cabinet submission is "soon" — and the regulator's most recent enforcement record makes clear what "soon" means in practice: 51 administrative penalties totalling R120 million in twelve months is not a warning shot. It is the marking guide for what post-COFI enforcement will look like at scale.

Why this week: The FSCA leadership transition of 31 May–1 June is the clearest institutional signal yet that COFI's implementation timeline is real. Ludin's departure removes the Deputy Commissioner who most directly represented the continuity of the pre-COFI regulatory approach. Kamlana's renewed mandate locks in an outcomes-focused regulatory philosophy for five years. Simultaneously, Santam's Lloyd's Syndicate 1918 — targeting £300–400 million GWP in 2026 — is the market's most significant capital allocation signal: SA's largest insurer is deploying underwriting capacity to London rather than Johannesburg. The SARB's revised macro outlook (inflation to 3.7%, one rate cut instead of two, fuel inflation at 18%) compounds the domestic stress environment into which COFI arrives.

Who wins, who loses: Fee-based independent advisers with documented outcomes records win — they are structurally COFI-compliant on Day 1 without remediation. Parametric product designers win — their automatic payout structures pass the fair outcomes test by construction. Tied-agent distribution models, commission-dependent bancassurance structures, and MNO-embedded products with 40% claims rejection rates lose — not immediately, but on a trajectory that the renewed FSCA mandate makes irreversible. The institutions that begin restructuring now have a 24-month lead. Those that wait for enforcement will restructure under regulatory pressure, at three times the cost.

§ 02
Black Swan Watch
Tail risks the industry is systematically underestimating
COFI Implementation Shock and the Intermediary Collapse Regulatory
Timeline: Short — 12–24 months from enactment
The Underestimation Argument

The industry's COFI risk model is calibrated on transition cost: compliance teams, system updates, legal reviews. It is not calibrated on the structural elimination of distribution economics. COFI's outcomes-based framework does not merely require fair treatment disclosures — it requires that the distribution model itself demonstrably produce fair customer outcomes. A tied-agent model that systematically replaces in-force policies, a bancassurance structure that bundles insurance without active consumer choice, or an MNO-embedded product with 40% claims rejection rates does not pass the outcomes test regardless of how well the documentation is structured. The industry has confused compliance preparation with outcomes preparation. These are not the same thing.

The Nonlinear Impact Scenario

COFI enacted in H2 2026 → FSCA begins outcomes-based supervisory reviews in Q1 2027 → first major finding against a top-10 life insurer's tied-agent model is published → the finding triggers a competitor review of all tied-agent structures → three life insurers simultaneously restructure their tied-agent compensation arrangements → 8,000–12,000 tied agents face materially reduced commission income → tied-agent lapse rates in group risk and individual life accelerate as advisers exit or reduce activity → life sector new business volumes fall 15–20% in the 18-month transition period.

Fragility Amplifiers

The FSCA has already demonstrated its enforcement willingness: 51 penalties totalling R120m in twelve months. Commissioner Kamlana's second term beginning 1 June 2026 resets the mandate clock — he has five years and one unfinished legislative project. The combination of renewed mandate and imminent COFI enactment creates the conditions for the most consequential enforcement cycle in SA financial services history.

Antifragility Opportunity

The antifragile distribution model for the COFI era is the fee-based, independent adviser who has no commission conflict of interest, maintains auditable outcome records, and can demonstrate fair treatment through a documented advice process. This model is currently a small minority of the intermediary market. Under COFI, it becomes the only model that is structurally compliant without structural remediation. Firms that have already built this model — or can acquire it — gain a distribution advantage that is reinforced by every enforcement action against commission-conflicted models.

SARB Hawkish Pivot: The Rate-Cut that Does Not Arrive Macro
Timeline: Short — 6–12 months
The Underestimation Argument

The insurance industry's reserve, pricing, and affordability models for 2026 were built in late 2025 when the SARB was projecting two rate cuts in 2026 and inflation of 3.3%. The March 2026 SARB MPC revised this to one cut and inflation of 3.7%, driven by fuel inflation exceeding 18%. The industry's embedded assumption — that consumer affordability stress would ease in H2 2026 as rates fell — is now materially wrong. The revised macro environment means lapse rates in personal lines, affordability-driven underinsurance in household cover, and claims frequency from financially distressed consumers will not improve on the previously modelled timeline. No major insurer has publicly updated its 2026 guidance to reflect the hawkish pivot.

The Nonlinear Impact Scenario

SARB holds repo at 6.75% through Q3 2026 as fuel-driven inflation exceeds 4% → consumer affordability stress deepens in Q2–Q3 → personal lines lapse rates for policies in the R800–R1,500/month bracket exceed 18% → direct personal lines underwriters face combined adverse selection (the most price-sensitive risks remain, the lowest-risk lapses leave) → motor claims frequency rises as financially stressed policyholders delay maintenance → household underinsurance gap widens as policyholders reduce sums insured at renewal to maintain affordability → two personal lines insurers report combined ratios above 100% for the first time since 2022.

Fragility Amplifiers

Fuel inflation at 18% flows directly into motor repair costs (parts transport), building repair costs (materials delivery), and food price CPI which drives the consumer distress accelerating lapse rates. The SA motor fleet's import dependency means the rand's sensitivity to Middle East conflict compounds the fuel-driven inflation impact specifically for the insurance cost line.

Antifragility Opportunity

The insurer that builds a dynamic lapse-prevention model — identifying at-risk policyholders before they lapse using claims frequency, payment behaviour, and sum-insured adequacy signals — and intervenes with a targeted affordability product (quarterly billing, reduced sum insured with explicit underinsurance acknowledgment, telematics discount) retains the book that competitors lose. The retention economics are asymmetric: preventing a lapse costs less than re-acquiring the same risk.

Santam Syndicate 1918 and the Offshore Capital Drain Market
Timeline: Medium — 18–36 months
The Underestimation Argument

The market is reading Santam Syndicate 1918 as a growth story — SA's largest insurer accessing new international markets. The underestimated reading is structural: Santam has concluded that £300–400m of underwriting capital generates better risk-adjusted returns at Lloyd's than in the domestic SA market. This is a rational response to deteriorating domestic combined ratios driven by climate events, fraud, and reinsurance cost increases — but it is also a signal that the domestic underwriting environment has become structurally less attractive to SA's most sophisticated underwriter. The market will take 18 months to absorb what this signal means for domestic pricing discipline.

The Nonlinear Impact Scenario

Santam Syndicate 1918 begins accepting SA CAT and specialty risks as inward reinsurance from July 2026 → Santam reduces its outward cession to Munich Re SA and Hannover Re SA at July renewal → global reinsurers respond by tightening terms for remaining SA cedants who have smaller portfolios and less sophisticated risk selection → domestic treaty market concentrates around fewer capacity providers at higher pricing → mid-market commercial insurers face a 20–35% effective reinsurance cost increase by January 2027 → two mid-tier short-term insurers begin strategic review discussions with larger groups.

Fragility Amplifiers

Santam's move is the leading indicator. If OUTsurance or Hollard follow with similar offshore capacity strategies, the domestic reinsurance market loses its three largest cedants simultaneously — a scenario for which the global reinsurers serving SA have no contingency pricing. The Lloyd's market's appetite for SA risk is now inversely correlated with the domestic market's stability: as SA risk worsens, Santam's syndicate becomes more valuable as a hedge, accelerating the capital drain from domestic deployment.

Antifragility Opportunity

The gap Santam's outward cession reduction creates for Munich Re SA and Hannover Re SA is filled by whichever domestic insurer moves fastest to offer multi-year treaties with SA-specific political risk overlays — creating a niche capacity play that does not exist at scale in the current market.

COFI and the Broker Existential Reckoning Regulatory
Timeline: Short to Medium — 18–30 months
The Underestimation Argument

South Africa has approximately 11,000 licensed FSPs operating as insurance intermediaries. COFI's activity-based framework does not licence FSPs — it licences activities. Every broker, every tied agent, every cell captive manager, and every binder holder will need to re-examine whether their current regulatory authorisation covers their actual activities under the new framework. The FSCA has stated explicitly that existing licences will require re-evaluation against the COFI activity definitions. The industry's underestimation is the number of brokers who will discover that their current business model is not mappable to any single COFI activity category — requiring either structural change or exit. The FSCA's contingency plan — implementing market conduct frameworks compatible with COFI through existing legislation if COFI is delayed — means the compliance pressure arrives regardless of the Bill's parliamentary timeline.

The Nonlinear Impact Scenario

COFI enacted → FSCA begins a 3-year licensing re-evaluation process → 2,000–3,000 smaller FSPs cannot demonstrate that their business model meets COFI's activity definitions → FSCA issues a market guidance note requiring FSPs with sub-R5m annual revenue to demonstrate client outcome records or surrender their licences → accelerated broker consolidation → 5–7 major broker group acquisitions in 24 months → concentration risk in the intermediary channel increases → the industry's largest brokers (Alexander Forbes, Aon SA, Marsh SA) capture 45–50% of commercial broker market share.

Fragility Amplifiers

The FSCA's enforcement record demonstrates that the regulator will pursue small intermediaries as readily as large ones — the 51 penalties in twelve months include debarments of individuals and small firm licence revocations. The broker consolidation this triggers is not merely a market efficiency adjustment — it is a structural reduction in advice diversity that may ultimately harm consumer outcomes in exactly the way COFI is designed to prevent.

Antifragility Opportunity

Broker consolidation creates an acquisition window for capitalised platform businesses. The value is not in the individual broker's revenue — it is in the client relationship records that, properly structured, demonstrate COFI-compliant outcomes history. A buyer who acquires three or four compliant small brokers and consolidates their client records onto a single outcomes-tracking platform creates a COFI-ready distribution asset worth 4–6x the sum of the individual businesses.

§ 04
Hidden Profit Pools
Where the real economics live — not where the headlines point
I
The COFI Compliance Infrastructure Market
First-mover advantage in a mandatory market
The Conventional View
COFI compliance is a cost centre. Every institution in the market faces the same compliance burden — legal review, system upgrades, training, governance documentation. There is no competitive advantage in compliance; there is only the risk of being caught non-compliant. The smart play is to do the minimum required and wait for clarity on enforcement priorities before investing heavily.
The Actual Economics
COFI compliance is only a cost centre for institutions that treat it as a legal project. For institutions that treat it as a customer outcomes infrastructure project, it becomes a revenue asset. The specific mechanism: COFI requires financial institutions to maintain outcome records — documented evidence that every customer interaction produced a fair result. Institutions that build outcome-tracking infrastructure now, before the requirement becomes mandatory, acquire three years of compliant outcome data before their competitors. This data becomes the basis for the FSCA's supervisory assessment — institutions with strong outcome data receive lighter-touch supervision. Those without it receive intrusive, resource-consuming reviews. The cost of intrusive supervision — management time, legal fees, remediation costs — is 8–15x the cost of building the infrastructure proactively. The profit pool is in the avoided supervision cost, not in any direct revenue line.
Why It Stays Hidden
The profit pool is invisible on a P&L because it is a cost avoidance rather than a revenue line. CFOs discount future avoided costs more heavily than future revenues, making the infrastructure investment difficult to justify in conventional capital allocation frameworks. The institutions that will capture this pool are those with CFOs who understand regulatory optionality pricing — the actuarial value of not being subject to an FSCA remediation order.
Who Is Best Positioned
Discovery Financial Services and Sanlam, both of which operate outcomes-tracking infrastructure for their Vitality and financial wellness programmes respectively, are closest to having the data architecture required. The insurer that can demonstrate to the FSCA that its customer interaction records already meet COFI's outcomes evidence standard — before COFI is enacted — gains a first-mover regulatory relationship advantage that takes competitors three years to replicate.
The Risk
If COFI's enactment is delayed beyond 2027 by parliamentary congestion — a plausible scenario given the GNU coalition dynamics — the institutions that invested early in compliance infrastructure carry a sunk cost that competitors avoided. The investment is a bet on the FSCA's political will to get COFI over the line on the current timeline.
II
The Broker Consolidation Acquisition Window
COFI-driven consolidation creates a 24-month buying opportunity
The Conventional View
Broker consolidation has been happening in SA for years. The major groups (Alexander Forbes, Aon, Marsh, Willis) have been acquiring smaller firms steadily. There is no specific urgency or structural opportunity that makes the next 24 months different from the previous 24.
The Actual Economics
COFI creates a specific, time-bounded acquisition catalyst that is structurally different from organic market consolidation. The mechanism: COFI's licensing re-evaluation process will require 2,000–3,000 smaller FSPs to demonstrate COFI-compliant business models. The FSPs that cannot make this demonstration — estimated at 15–25% of the licensed intermediary universe — will be forced sellers of their client books within 24–36 months of enactment. Forced seller dynamics produce compressed valuations: brokers who cannot survive the regulatory transition will accept 1.5–2x recurring revenue where the market in normal conditions clears at 2.5–3.5x. The window for acquiring client books at distressed multiples is the 18–24 months between COFI enactment and the FSCA's first licensing deadline. After that window closes, the surviving brokers have already captured the distressed books and valuations normalise.
Why It Stays Hidden
The opportunity requires reading COFI as a consolidation catalyst rather than a compliance project. The intermediary M&A advisory firms (corporate finance boutiques) have not modelled this dynamic because they price deals on current multiples, not on the regulatory optionality embedded in a forced-seller environment. The first buyer to recognise the forced-seller window will transact at the best pricing.
Who Is Best Positioned
Platform businesses with existing FSP licences, digital client management infrastructure, and access to acquisition capital. Specifically: insurtech platforms with binder authority and digital advice capability are the natural consolidators — they acquire the client relationships, absorb them onto their platform, and immediately generate COFI-compliant outcome records. The traditional broker groups are also well-positioned but move more slowly due to integration complexity.
The Risk
Client attrition from acquired books — clients may not follow their broker to a new platform. Retention rates on acquired broker books average 60–75% in normal conditions; distressed acquisitions during a regulatory transition may produce lower retention as clients take the opportunity to re-tender. The acquisition economics assume 70%+ retention.
III
Reinsurance Capacity for COFI-Compliant Product Structures
New product architecture creates new reinsurance treaty opportunities
The Conventional View
Reinsurance treaties are structured around existing product lines — motor, property, liability, life. COFI does not change the underlying risks being insured; it changes how they are distributed and disclosed. Reinsurance capacity is agnostic to regulatory frameworks.
The Actual Economics
COFI will require insurance products to be designed from the customer outcome backward — the product must demonstrably serve the customer's need before it can be marketed. This is the inverse of how most SA insurance products are currently designed (product first, distribution second, customer outcome as an afterthought). The new product architecture COFI requires will produce a different risk profile from existing products: better-selected customers (COFI's suitability requirements eliminate mismatched sales), cleaner claims experience (disclosed exclusions reduce disputed claims), and lower fraud incidence (COFI's record-keeping requirements create a paper trail that deters fraudulent claims). These are structural improvements to the underlying risk pool that should, over 3–5 years, produce better loss ratios. The reinsurance market has not yet priced this improvement into treaty terms for COFI-compliant product structures. The first cedant to demonstrate COFI-compliant product design and its loss ratio impact will negotiate materially better treaty terms — 5–8 percentage points of treaty pricing — than competitors writing structurally equivalent risk under non-compliant distribution.
Why It Stays Hidden
Global reinsurers price SA risk on historic loss data, which reflects pre-COFI product structures and distribution models. They have not modelled the loss ratio improvement that outcomes-based distribution produces. Munich Re and Swiss Re's SA underwriting teams do not yet have a framework for adjusting treaty pricing based on cedant COFI compliance status. The information asymmetry between COFI-compliant cedants and reinsurers who do not yet price compliance is the temporary profit window.
Who Is Best Positioned
The insurer that can present its reinsurance broker with a documented COFI compliance programme, a customer outcomes dashboard, and a 12-month forward loss ratio projection adjusted for COFI-related adverse selection reduction will be the first to negotiate compliance-based treaty discounts. This is a two- to three-year window before all cedants are COFI-compliant and the differentiation disappears.
The Risk
The loss ratio improvement from COFI-compliant distribution requires 3–5 years of experience data to be credible to reinsurers. In the interim, the cedant must fund the compliance investment without the treaty pricing benefit. The investment is a bet on the long-run economics of better-designed products — a bet that only patient capital can make.

§ 5A — Life Insurance
The Longevity Trade
Contrarian Insight
The life insurance industry believes COFI's biggest threat is product disclosure complexity. The actual threat is distribution economics. COFI's outcomes-based framework makes commission-driven replacement business structurally non-compliant — not because replacement is prohibited, but because systematic replacement that benefits agents rather than clients fails the fair outcome test. SA life insurers derive 15–25% of new business volumes from product replacement. This revenue line is not mapped in any COFI risk assessment the industry has published, because it requires acknowledging that a significant portion of current distribution economics is built on outcomes that COFI defines as unfair.
Risk Scenario
The SARB hawkish pivot — inflation revised to 3.7%, only one rate cut projected — extends the consumer affordability stress that is accelerating lapse rates in the R800–R1,500/month premium bracket. A COFI-enactment shock arriving into an elevated lapse environment produces a compound new business drought: fewer new policies being written (tied agents restructuring), higher lapses on in-force policies (affordability stress), and a claims pipeline that continues regardless. A major life insurer reports new business volumes 25% below budget for Q3 2026, triggering an earnings guidance revision.
Opportunity
Fee-based independent financial advisers with documented client outcome records are structurally COFI-compliant on Day 1 of enactment. Life insurers that build dedicated distribution partnerships with fee-based IFA networks — rather than expanding tied-agent capacity — acquire COFI-compliant distribution that does not require restructuring when enforcement begins. Discovery Life's independent broker channel is already this model at scale. The life insurers that will lose most under COFI are those most invested in the tied-agent model they have not yet started to restructure.
§ 5B — Health / Medical Schemes
Healthcare Convexity
Contrarian Insight
Medical scheme administrators are focused on NHI as their existential regulatory threat. The COFI threat is more immediate and less discussed. COFI's activity-based framework will require medical scheme administrators — who currently operate under the Medical Schemes Act rather than FAIS — to reassess whether their ancillary financial services activities (gap cover intermediation, wellness product bundling, employer benefit advisory) require COFI authorisation. Several major scheme administrators provide services that, under COFI's activity definitions, constitute financial advice or distribution of financial products. The re-licensing requirement could require scheme administrators to restructure their ancillary revenue lines — which collectively represent 8–12% of total revenue — within the transition period.
Risk Scenario
NHI ConCourt judgment pending simultaneously with COFI enactment creates a dual regulatory uncertainty that is operationally paralyzing. Scheme administrators cannot complete COFI compliance planning for their product ranges while the NHI judgment holds open the possibility that benefit structures will be mandatorily redesigned. The administrators most at risk are those who have delayed COFI compliance planning on the basis that NHI uncertainty makes it pointless to plan for a product range that may be legally voided.
Opportunity
The COFI transition creates a product rationalisation opportunity that scheme administrators should be executing voluntarily before being required to. Medical schemes currently offer between 8 and 18 benefit options — many of which are held open for regulatory reasons rather than commercial demand. COFI's product governance requirements will require administrators to justify each benefit option against a fair value assessment. The administrators that proactively rationalise to 4–6 genuinely differentiated options, with documented fair value assessments, will be COFI-ready and operationally leaner than those who wait.
§ 5C — Short-Term: Personal Lines
Everyday Risk Is Not Normal
Contrarian Insight
Personal lines insurers are treating COFI as a disclosure and documentation challenge. The actual COFI challenge for short-term insurance is pricing transparency. COFI's fair treatment framework will require insurers to demonstrate that their pricing models do not systematically disadvantage identifiable customer segments — particularly lower-income customers, older customers, and customers in high-risk geographies who currently pay premiums that reflect concentrated adverse selection rather than their individual risk profiles. The practice of geographic loading, age-based loading, and credit-score-linked pricing will be reviewed against COFI's outcomes principles. Not all of these practices will survive that review unchanged.
Risk Scenario
AI-generated fraud — already identified as an active threat in Issue 003 — intensifies under COFI's automated claims processing scrutiny. COFI's requirement that algorithmic decisions be explainable means that automated claims approval systems must be able to demonstrate why each claim was approved or denied. Insurers who have automated claims approval without explainable AI models face a regulatory gap that is simultaneously a fraud vulnerability and a COFI compliance failure. A major fraud event on an automated claims channel during the COFI transition becomes a compound regulatory and operational crisis.
Opportunity
Telematics penetration in SA personal lines motor stands at approximately 18% of active policies. COFI's product governance requirements create a specific telematics opportunity: a telematics-based motor product can demonstrate fair value more clearly than any conventional actuarial pricing model, because the premium is directly linked to observable behaviour. The insurer that files a COFI-compliant telematics product with the FSCA early in the transition period creates a product category precedent and acquires the regulatory relationship advantage of being the first mover.
§ 5D — Commercial Insurance
Corporate Fragility Map
Contrarian Insight
Commercial insurance brokers believe COFI primarily affects personal lines — the regulated consumer market. This is wrong. COFI's sophisticated counterparty exemptions are narrower than the industry assumes. Many mid-market commercial clients — SMEs with turnover below R50 million — will not qualify for sophisticated counterparty exemptions, meaning their insurance brokers are subject to the same outcomes framework as personal lines advisers. The commercial broker market has not modelled the proportion of its client book that falls below the sophisticated counterparty threshold, because this analysis has not been required before. When COFI forces this categorisation, a significant share of commercial brokers will discover they have been operating personal-lines-equivalent obligations toward clients they have been treating as commercial counterparties.
Risk Scenario
Business insolvency projections of 1,540 cases in 2026 are creating a live claims pipeline in D&O, trade credit, and business interruption. The COFI transition absorbs broker and insurer management attention at precisely the moment when the claims environment demands operational focus. The compound risk is operational distraction: COFI compliance programmes are consuming senior management bandwidth that should be focused on emerging claims trends, leaving insurers less agile in responding to a deteriorating commercial claims environment.
Opportunity
COFI-compliant commercial broker advice for mid-market SME clients — with documented needs analysis, documented product alternatives, and documented fair value assessment — creates a professional liability shield that non-compliant brokers lack. In a rising insolvency environment where clients are more likely to contest denied claims or seek advice liability, the broker with documented COFI-compliant advice records is structurally protected. This is both a compliance investment and a professional indemnity risk management tool.
§ 5E — Specialised Insurance
Edge Markets
Contrarian Insight
The specialised insurance market assumes that COFI's sophisticated counterparty exemptions will effectively exclude Lloyd's-placed marine, aviation, energy, and political risk business from the outcomes framework. This exemption is more fragile than the market believes. COFI's activity-based framework defines sophistication at the transaction level, not the client level. A sophisticated corporate client buying D&O cover may be sophisticated for that transaction but not for a first-time parametric weather product. The insurer or broker that extends sophisticated counterparty treatment to all transactions with a large corporate client — rather than assessing it transaction by transaction — will face an FSCA challenge when the corporate client lodges a complaint on a product they did not understand.
Risk Scenario
Santam Syndicate 1918's entry into Lloyd's specialty markets with £300–400m GWP creates a new competitive dynamic in SA-exposed specialty lines. Santam can now offer internationally rated capacity for SA specialty risks that previously required London market placement — potentially compressing margins in the specialty niche by bringing SA-specific underwriting knowledge into competition with London market generalists.
Opportunity
Political risk underwriting for domestic SA corporates — identified in Issue 003 as an underserved opportunity — is explicitly carved out of COFI's simplified treatment provisions. Political risk for domestic corporates is a COFI-era product design opportunity: it is complex, high-value, requires genuine professional advice, and fits cleanly into COFI's sophisticated counterparty framework. The first Lloyd's coverholder with a COFI-structured domestic political risk product has an uncontested market for 18–24 months.
§ 5F — Reinsurance
The System Behind the System
Contrarian Insight
The SA reinsurance market is reading Santam Syndicate 1918 as competition — a new Lloyd's capacity provider entering markets where Munich Re SA and Hannover Re SA currently dominate. The more accurate reading is that Santam has become a reinsurance buyer and seller simultaneously, creating a natural internal hedging capability that reduces its dependency on the domestic treaty market. The competitive threat to local reinsurers is not that Santam will undercut their pricing in SA commercial risks — it is that Santam's offshore syndicate reduces its outward cession requirements, making it a smaller, more selective buyer of reinsurance capacity. The reinsurers who have built their SA book around Santam's volume will find their renewal conversations in July 2026 materially different from those in January 2026.
Risk Scenario
COFI's product governance requirements will change the design of insurance products that underlie reinsurance treaties. If primary insurers redesign personal lines products with more explicit exclusions and better fair value documentation, the underlying risk pool changes — potentially reducing adverse selection and improving loss ratios, but also reducing premium volumes as marginal risks are priced out of compliant products. Treaty pricing that was calibrated on current premium volumes may become uneconomic if COFI-driven product rationalisation reduces cession volumes by 10–15% across product lines.
Opportunity
The first global reinsurer to publish a COFI compliance premium — a treaty pricing adjustment that explicitly rewards SA cedants for COFI-compliant product design and distribution — captures the market intelligence on which SA cedants are genuinely outcomes-based and which are merely documented as such. This pricing differentiation becomes a data advantage: the reinsurer that prices COFI compliance earliest has the best data on which institutions are structurally well-run versus those that are regulatory-arbitraging the transition period.
§ 5G — Microinsurance
Mass Market Experiments
Contrarian Insight
COFI is widely described as the biggest threat to the microinsurance market's current MNO-embedded distribution model. The contrarian reading is that COFI eliminates the competitive moat that incumbents have built on regulatory opacity — and the biggest winner is the new entrant with an outcomes-first design. The current MNO-embedded model's 38–44% claims rejection rate is not a design feature that any new entrant would replicate. A microinsurance product designed from the start around COFI's outcomes principles — transparent triggers, automatic payouts, zero exclusions for standard risks — is structurally superior to the existing model and gains regulatory tailwind rather than headwind from COFI enactment.
Risk Scenario
COFI's enactment triggers a mandatory re-evaluation of credit life products embedded in BNPL and informal lending channels — the fastest-growing microinsurance distribution rail. Credit life attachment rates above 90% in informal lending will be flagged as presumptively non-compliant with COFI's suitability requirements. An FSCA sweep of BNPL-linked credit life products finds systematic mis-selling at scale across 4–5 million policyholders. The remediation liability — refund of premiums where no valid claims basis existed — exceeds the net profit margins of three microinsurance carriers.
Opportunity
Parametric microinsurance products — weather triggers, power outage triggers, municipal service failure triggers — are structurally COFI-compliant because the payout mechanism is objective and automatic: no claims assessment, no discretion, no denial. The FSCA's outcomes framework cannot find an unfair outcome in a product that pays automatically when an objective trigger is met. The first parametric microinsurance product that graduates from the FSCA sandbox into full commercial launch during the COFI transition period becomes the benchmark against which all competing microinsurance products are assessed.
§ 5H — Insurtech / Emerging Models
Zero-to-One Insurance
Contrarian Insight
SA insurtechs are treating COFI as an incumbents' problem — the big life and short-term insurers with legacy distribution models and commission-dependent agents face the real structural challenge, while insurtechs with digital-first, customer-centric models are already COFI-aligned. This is a dangerous misreading. COFI's algorithmic decisioning provisions apply with particular force to digital-native underwriting models, and the requirement to explain every automated decision to a standard that a retail customer can understand is operationally more demanding for ML-based underwriting than for rule-based actuarial pricing. The compliance gap for insurtechs is not in their customer proposition — it is in their explainability infrastructure.
Risk Scenario
COFI's licensing re-evaluation will require insurtechs operating under binder authority or cell captive arrangements to re-examine whether their current licence structures are mappable to COFI activity definitions. The most exposed are insurtechs that have built hybrid models — part underwriter, part intermediary, part administrator — to optimise capital efficiency. COFI's activity-based framework does not accommodate hybrid models that exist primarily for regulatory arbitrage. An FSCA re-evaluation finding that an insurtech's current structure requires three separate COFI licences rather than one is not merely a compliance cost — it is a business model restructuring requirement.
Opportunity
COFI explicitly enables open finance and API-based distribution — the activity-based framework allows financial services to be distributed through any channel that can demonstrate fair customer outcomes, without requiring a traditional FSP licence for the distribution channel itself. This is the legislative foundation for true embedded insurance: a retailer, telco, or fintech can embed insurance distribution into its customer journey under a COFI authorisation framework that did not exist under FAIS. The first insurtech to secure a COFI-compliant embedded distribution authorisation for a major non-financial platform partner creates a distribution moat that incumbents cannot match through their existing FSP licence structures.
§ 06
Second-Order Effects
The causal chain beyond the obvious headline
This Month's Trend
The COFI Bill approaching Cabinet enactment — FSCA Commissioner Kamlana's second five-year mandate beginning 1 June 2026, Deputy Commissioner Ludin's departure on 31 May, and the regulator's demonstrated enforcement appetite of R120 million in penalties in twelve months.
First-Order Effects — Obvious (0–6 months)

The industry acknowledges that COFI is imminent. Legal teams are reviewing existing product terms against COFI principles. Compliance officers are preparing gap analyses. Governance committees are being briefed. The FSCA has indicated the Bill will reach Cabinet "soon." Commissioner Kamlana's reappointment for five years signals regulatory continuity. The market-facing narrative is: "We are prepared. The transition period gives us time to adjust. This is managed change." This is the comfortable first-order reading.

Second-Order Effects — Non-Obvious (6–24 months)

The discovery that preparation is not the same as compliance. Legal gap analyses identify product wording issues and disclosure deficiencies — these are fixable within the transition period. What gap analyses do not identify are systematic poor customer outcomes embedded in distribution models that are structurally incentive-misaligned. A tied-agent model where 60% of new business involves replacing in-force policies passes every legal review because no rule prohibits replacement. Under COFI's outcomes framework, it fails the fair treatment test because the replacement demonstrably benefits the agent (new commission) rather than the client (equivalent cover, new surrender period, new exclusion period). The FSCA's supervisory reviews will find this within 18 months of enactment. The legal team's gap analysis will not have flagged it because it is not a legal gap — it is a business model design problem.

The intermediary crisis. COFI's activity-based licensing framework will trigger a re-evaluation of approximately 11,000 FSP licences against new activity definitions. The FSCA has signalled that its contingency plan — implementing COFI-compatible frameworks through existing legislation if the Bill is delayed — means the licensing pressure arrives regardless of parliamentary timing. FSPs who cannot demonstrate that their business model fits an activity definition will be pressed to restructure, merge, or exit. The broker consolidation this triggers will be faster and more chaotic than the market currently models, because it will be driven by regulatory pressure rather than commercial logic.

Reinsurance repricing for non-compliant cedants. As FSCA enforcement actions begin identifying specific insurers with poor customer outcomes, global reinsurers will start requesting COFI compliance status as part of treaty renewal due diligence. Munich Re and Swiss Re already include regulatory compliance questionnaires in their renewal processes for some jurisdictions. COFI will add a SA-specific compliance dimension within 18–24 months of enactment. Non-compliant cedants will face a reinsurance market that is beginning to price regulatory risk — an additional 3–5 percentage points on treaty pricing that the domestic market does not currently model.

Third-Order Effects — Structural Implications (2–7 years)

COFI will produce two SA insurance markets: a compliant market operating on outcomes-based economics, and a rump non-compliant market contracting under enforcement pressure. The compliant market will have structurally better loss ratios (better-selected customers, cleaner claims), lower distribution costs (consolidation has eliminated 3,000–4,000 marginal FSPs), and more transparent product economics. The non-compliant rump will have worse loss ratios, increasing regulatory costs, and shrinking distribution capacity as intermediaries exit or get debarred.

The structural winner is not necessarily the largest institution — it is the institution that most quickly redesigns its business model around customer outcomes rather than product sales. Discovery Financial Services, with its Vitality ecosystem and documented engagement data, is structurally closer to COFI compliance than any traditional life insurer. The traditional life sector's tied-agent model, bancassurance bundling, and commission-driven group risk business will require the most structural redesign — and will take the longest to achieve genuine compliance rather than documented compliance.

In seven years, the SA insurance industry will look like the post-RDR UK market: fewer intermediaries, higher professional standards, transparent fees replacing commissions on most product lines, and a consolidation of distribution into a small number of platform businesses that dominate advice delivery. The institutions that built for this outcome in 2026–2027 will own the market structure that emerges.

The Strategic Pivot Point
Does the SA insurance industry treat COFI as a legal compliance deadline to be managed — adjusting documentation, updating disclosures, and waiting for the transition period to expire — or as an operational redesign mandate that requires rebuilding distribution economics from the customer outcome backward? The institutions that answer this question correctly in the next 12 months will define the competitive landscape for the following decade.
§ 07
Strategic Recommendations
Specific moves available now — informed by this month's intelligence
For Insurers
Commission a Customer Outcomes Audit Before COFI Enactment
Engage an independent actuarial firm to conduct a customer outcomes audit of your three highest-volume distribution channels — tied agents, bancassurance, and direct digital — against COFI's draft outcomes principles. The audit should specifically test for: replacement business rates in tied-agent channels, product suitability match rates in bancassurance, and claims denial rates in digital channels. These are the three areas the FSCA has indicated it will prioritise in its first supervisory review cycle. An internal audit commissioned before enactment is privileged; an FSCA-initiated review is not.
Why now: COFI enactment is weeks to months away. The FSCA's enforcement track record — 51 penalties totalling R120m in twelve months — is the best available signal of its post-COFI supervisory appetite. A proactive internal audit and remediation plan is worth significantly more than a reactive FSCA remediation order.
Redesign One Product Line Using Outcomes-First Architecture
Select your highest-volume personal lines or credit life product and redesign it from scratch using COFI's outcomes-based product governance framework: define the target market, the fair value assessment, the distribution requirements, and the outcome metrics before writing the product wording. This single redesign exercise will produce three outputs: a COFI-compliant product that can be cited as evidence of readiness, an internal understanding of what outcomes-first design requires, and a product wording that reduces claims disputes because exclusions are disclosed meaningfully rather than buried.
Why now: The institutions that have one COFI-designed product on the market when the Bill is enacted demonstrate to the FSCA that their compliance is operational, not aspirational. This is a supervisory relationship asset worth more than any compliance programme documentation.
Build the Treaty Compliance Case for Reinsurers Now
Prepare a COFI compliance brief for your reinsurance renewal submissions at July 2026 — not because reinsurers are yet requiring it, but because being the first cedant to present it creates a differentiation that persists for 3–5 years. The brief should document your COFI readiness programme, your customer outcomes audit results, and your projected loss ratio improvement from COFI-driven adverse selection reduction. Munich Re SA and Hannover Re SA will respond to this briefing with interest — their global treaty pricing teams are beginning to develop COFI pricing adjustments and need data to calibrate them.
Why now: The July 2026 renewal is 8 weeks away. The first cedant to present a COFI compliance brief creates the benchmark that all subsequent briefs are measured against.
For Brokers
Conduct a COFI Activity Mapping Audit on Your Licence
Map every revenue-generating activity your business performs against the COFI draft activity definitions. The question is not whether you are currently authorised to do these activities — you are. The question is whether each activity, as you perform it, fits a COFI activity definition as the FSCA intends it. Pay particular attention to: investment advice bundled with insurance placement, binder authority that extends beyond pure claims administration, and cell captive participation that blurs the lines between underwriting and intermediation.
Why now: The FSCA has confirmed it will conduct a re-evaluation of all existing FSP licences against COFI activity definitions during the transition period. Discovering a misalignment during a voluntary audit costs significantly less than discovering it during an FSCA supervisory review.
Build a Client Outcomes Dashboard Before It Is Required
Implement a client relationship management system that tracks, for every client interaction: the advice given, the product recommended, the alternatives presented, and the outcome achieved (renewal, claim paid, lapse). This is not yet required but will be under COFI. Brokers who already have three years of outcomes data when the FSCA begins supervisory reviews will receive significantly lighter-touch oversight than those who are building the system in response to a supervisory request.
Why now: Data accumulation is time-dependent. Starting in May 2026 means 18–24 months of outcomes data by the time FSCA supervisory reviews begin. Starting in 2027 means starting from zero under regulatory scrutiny.
Position as a COFI-Ready Acquirer of Non-Compliant Broker Books
Identify five to ten smaller FSPs in your market segment whose business model is non-compliant with COFI's anticipated outcomes requirements. Approach them now — before the FSCA begins its licensing re-evaluation — and offer to acquire their client books at a premium to the distressed valuation they will receive in 18 months. The book is more valuable to you now (pre-distress, full retention, no regulatory pressure) than it will be post-enactment. Price the deal with a two-year retention earnout to align incentives and manage the client attrition risk.
Why now: The acquisition window opens when COFI is enacted and the forced-seller dynamic begins. The negotiating advantage belongs entirely to the buyer who engages before the seller is under regulatory pressure.
For Regulators
FSCA: Publish a COFI Implementation Roadmap on Day One of Enactment
The single most disruptive feature of COFI's transition will not be the legislation itself — it will be interpretive uncertainty. What exactly does 'fair customer outcome' mean for a specific product category? How will the FSCA assess outcomes in tied-agent distribution versus independent advice? When the Bill is enacted, the FSCA should simultaneously publish a product-category-specific outcomes framework — not the principles, but the specific metrics it will use in supervisory reviews. This eliminates the interpretive arbitrage that allows non-compliant institutions to claim they are compliant while waiting for enforcement to define the standard.
Why now: The FSCA's own 2026 Conference noted that readiness is an industry-wide imperative. The regulator cannot demand industry readiness without providing the specific outcome metrics that readiness requires.
PA: Revise ORSA Stress Scenarios to Include COFI Business Model Disruption
The current SAM ORSA framework stress scenarios cover market, credit, insurance, and operational risks. COFI's distribution disruption — specifically the potential lapse acceleration from tied-agent restructuring and intermediary consolidation — is a business model risk that no current ORSA scenario captures. The PA should issue guidance requiring all life insurers and composite insurers with tied-agent distribution to include a COFI Distribution Shock scenario: a 15–20% reduction in new business volumes sustained for 24 months as distribution channels restructure. This is a plausible, foreseeable stress that the PA has the mandate to require insurers to model.
Why now: ORSA stress scenarios must be updated within 12 months of a material change in the business environment. COFI enactment is a material change. Waiting for the first lapse acceleration before requiring the scenario to be modelled means acting after the event.
FSCA: Create a COFI Compliance Safe Harbour for Voluntary Early Disclosures
The FSCA should announce a 12-month safe harbour: any institution that voluntarily discloses a COFI-incompatible product or distribution practice before a specified date, and submits a remediation plan, will not be subject to the maximum administrative penalty for the disclosed practice. This is the most powerful tool available to accelerate genuine compliance — it converts the decision to remediate proactively from a financial risk into a financial benefit. The FSCA's enforcement record demonstrates it can impose significant penalties. A safe harbour converts that credible threat into a positive incentive for the institutions most in need of remediation.
Why now: Safe harbours must be announced before non-compliance is discovered, not after. A safe harbour announced after the FSCA has begun supervisory reviews has no incentive effect — it only benefits institutions that have already been caught.
§ 08
The Contrarian Take
Thiel Mode — what important truth does almost nobody in the industry agree with?
⊞ The Consensus Belief Being Challenged
COFI is a long-delayed piece of legislation that the industry has absorbed and prepared for. The framework principles are well understood, compliance programmes are underway, and the transition period will give the market time to adjust. This is managed regulatory change, not disruption.

The insurance industry believes COFI is a compliance challenge it will manage through legal review and documentation. The uncomfortable truth is that COFI is a business model audit — and the audit has already started. The FSCA imposed 51 administrative penalties totalling R120 million in twelve months under the existing regulatory framework. Under COFI's outcomes-based standard, the institutions that received those penalties were not unlucky — they were operating business models that systematically produced unfair customer outcomes. COFI does not change what the regulator is looking for. It changes the legal standard under which it can act.

The specific error the industry is making is treating COFI as a disclosure standard when it is an outcomes standard. Updating product wordings, enhancing disclosure documents, and training distribution staff to explain exclusions more clearly will produce COFI-compliant documentation. It will not produce COFI-compliant outcomes if the underlying distribution model is structurally incentive-misaligned. A tied-agent who replaces 60% of in-force policies because commission is paid on new business rather than on client retention is not creating fair customer outcomes regardless of how clearly the replacement is disclosed. COFI's outcomes framework will find this. The FSCA's supervisory methodology — comparing claims experience, lapse rates, complaints data, and replacement business rates against sector benchmarks — is designed precisely to detect distribution models where outcomes are poor even when documentation is clean.

The strategic implication is urgent and uncomfortable: the institutions that will face the most disruptive COFI remediation orders are not the marginal operators who were already struggling with compliance. They are the major institutions whose commission-driven distribution models have been the most profitable — and whose profitability was, in part, a function of the outcomes those models produced. Commissioner Kamlana has five years. The enforcement record of the past twelve months suggests he intends to use them.

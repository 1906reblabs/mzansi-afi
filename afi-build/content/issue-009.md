    tag: "Regulatory"
    timeline: "Short — already materialising"
    underestimation: >-
      The FSCA published the draft OMNI-Risk Return and Explanatory Guide on 30 September
      2025 under Communication 19 of 2025, ran a two-month consultation to 30 November
      2025, and scheduled an industry pilot for mid-2026 ahead of a full IRS go-live in
      September 2026 — this month. The Return replaces the old Omni-Conduct of Business
      Return and is structured across twelve sections covering group structure and
      beneficial ownership, geographical footprint, governance (board composition,
      remuneration, non-compliance tolerance, insurance and guarantee claims), customer
      base and politically exposed persons, and more. Industry commentary through 2026
      treated the consultation and pilot phases as the main event; the actual go-live,
      arriving now, has drawn comparatively little coverage relative to its supervisory
      significance — every regulated institution is handing the FSCA a comparable,
      standardised risk profile for the first time in the regulator's history.
    scenario: >-
      An insurer's OMNI-Risk Return — submitted without the internal governance review this
      publication has recommended since Issue 006 — feeds into the IRS's automated risk
      model and generates a supervisory flag the institution did not anticipate, because
      nobody inside the business modelled what the twelve sections would show in aggregate
      before submitting them separately, department by department. The FSCA's supervisory
      attention, no longer dependent on a specific incident crystallising as it was with
      Banxso, turns to the flagged institution on the strength of its own governance data.
    amplifiers: >-
      The Return explicitly captures governance non-compliance tolerance and remediation
      history, alongside insurance and guarantee claims data at board level — meaning
      institutions that have treated governance documentation as a retrospective exercise,
      rather than a contemporaneous one, are submitting a comparatively weaker picture of
      themselves than institutions that have not.
    opportunity: >-
      An insurer that runs its own internal OMNI-Risk Return simulation before the formal
      submission — modelling what its twelve-section aggregate profile will show a
      regulator seeing it for the first time — can identify and address governance gaps on
      its own timeline rather than the FSCA's.

    subtitle: "Modelling your own supervisory risk score before the regulator does"
    conventional_view: >-
      The OMNI-Risk Return is being treated internally, where it is discussed at all, as a
      data-mapping and systems exercise — assembling twelve sections' worth of governance,
      ownership, geographic and customer data into the FSCA's required format, owned by
      compliance and IT rather than by risk or the executive committee.
    actual_economics: >-
      The Return is the input to the FSCA's new automated supervisory risk model under the
      IRS, going live this month — the first time the regulator will hold a single,
      standardised, comparable risk profile for every institution it supervises, rather
      than fragmented, licence-by-licence assessments. An institution that runs its own
      internal simulation of what its aggregate twelve-section profile will show — before
      submitting it — can identify and close governance gaps documented informally across
      this publication's last three issues (anchored renewal pricing, AI vendor
      concentration, absent NHI transition protocols, board-level non-compliance
      tolerance) on its own timeline, rather than discovering them via an FSCA-initiated
      review the Return itself triggered.
    why_hidden: >-
      The advantage is invisible from outside the institution — no competitor can observe
      whether a rival has run an internal readiness simulation before submitting, and the
      FSCA has given no indication it will publish comparative OMNI-Risk Return scores.
    best_positioned: >-
      An insurer or group with existing risk-governance infrastructure — an
      assumption-challenge process, a documented AI-bias-testing protocol, a
      vendor-dependency map — of the kind this publication has recommended building since
      Issue 006 has the readiness simulation largely built already; it simply needs to be
      pointed at the Return's twelve sections rather than treated as new work.
    risk: >-
      Once the IRS has been live for a full supervisory cycle and institutions understand
      empirically what triggers a flagged review, the first-mover readiness advantage
      compresses into a standard compliance step that every institution performs before
      submission — likely within twelve to eighteen months, consistent with the erosion
      pattern this publication has documented for comparable governance-disclosure
      windows.

## Executive Brief

South Africa's insurers are this month handing their regulator something the FSCA has never had before: a single, standardised, machine-readable risk profile, submitted through the OMNI-Risk Return and fed into the live Integrated Regulatory Solution (IRS), scheduled for full go-live in September 2026. The Return applies to every FSCA-regulated institution — insurers, retirement funds, advisers, administrators — and its deadline does not wait for the COFI Bill, still only at an earlier stage in Parliament.

**Why it matters this month:** the go-live lands in the same week the Board of Healthcare Funders, representing roughly half of South Africa's medical scheme beneficiaries, formally challenged the Council for Medical Schemes' 2027 contribution guidance — a public dispute that exists because the CMS, unlike the FSCA from this month, has no systemic way to see which schemes are exceeding its guidance in aggregate before one reaches curatorship, as Sizwe Hosmed did in September 2025.

**Who wins, who loses:** insurers that have already built the governance processes this publication has described across Issues 006 through 008 — assumption-challenge panels, vendor-dependency mapping, documented AI explainability — are submitting a Return that reflects real discipline. Insurers filling in the Return as a data-mapping exercise are handing the FSCA an accurate picture of exactly the gaps this publication has documented, before doing anything to close them.

## The Contrarian Take

**The contrarian claim:** the Return is not a bigger form. It is the first instrument that lets the FSCA compare one insurer's governance quality against every other regulated entity in the country, continuously, without needing a complaint or an incident to direct its attention — converting South African financial supervision from a reactive, complaint-and-incident-driven model, the model that produced the Banxso enforcement action this publication analysed in Issue 008, to a comparative one. Institutions treating the Return as a data-mapping exercise are, in effect, volunteering the evidence for exactly the governance gaps this publication has spent three issues documenting.

The sharpest evidence: the Return's twelve sections explicitly capture governance non-compliance tolerance and remediation history at board level — not merely operational metrics — meaning institutions submit a direct account of their own governance discipline, not a proxy for it. The IRS is designed, in the FSCA's own consultation language, to produce a single, comparable risk profile for each financial institution regardless of how many licences it holds — an explicit framing of cross-institution comparison, not institution-by-institution assessment. And the go-live proceeds on its own September 2026 timeline regardless of where COFI sits in the parliamentary process — the Return is not contingent on COFI's own slower legislative path, meaning institutions cannot treat it as part of a longer, still-negotiable reform horizon.

**The strategic implication:** every institution that has treated the governance recommendations in this publication's last three issues — assumption-challenge panels, AI bias-testing protocols, vendor-dependency mapping, fronting-relationship documentation — as optional, forward-looking best practice rather than urgent, now has a concrete, dated reason those recommendations were never optional: the regulator is switching on, this month, the first tool capable of seeing whether an institution built them or not.

## Closing Line

*For the first time, South Africa's insurance regulator does not need an incident to see a governance gap — it only needs the form every insurer is filling in this month.*
